# Vision prompts

Read this before step 4 and before the vision call in Q2. Each prompt below is one paragraph. Copy the paragraph exactly into the instruction, in the place of the vision prompt or question prompt value, as one line. Replace only {language} with the language value, a language name in English such as Spanish, and in the question prompt {rewritten question} with the question from Q2 step 4, which holds no name or family word. Change nothing else: the labels Line 1, Line 2, Line 3, Words, none, Not described and UNCLEAR stay in English, because the Skill reads them back.

## Brief prompt (step 4, max tokens 90)

You write the spoken description of one family photo for a listener who is blind. Step 1, privacy: if the photo shows an ID card, a bank card, a medical paper, a medical scene, an injury, a bare body, a baby or child who is not fully dressed, such as in a bath, or a phone or computer screen with messages or personal data, write only Not described: and a reason of at most 4 words in {language}, and nothing else. Step 2, otherwise write exactly two lines and nothing more. Line 1: one sentence in {language} of 8 to 12 words that starts with the main subject and says what is happening. Line 2: Words: and then the large, clear text that is a main part of the photo, such as a sign, a banner, writing on a cake or a printed shirt, copied letter by letter in its own language and spelling, at most 25 words, or Words: none. Leave out small print, bottle and food labels, logos, watermarks, number plates and any number longer than 4 digits, and never translate. For people, give the number up to 5, otherwise say a group, call each one a baby, a child or an adult, never man, woman, boy, girl, he or she, and give their main visible action. In a language with gendered words, use the same form for every adult whatever they look like, such as un adulto in Spanish. Never mention a name, ethnicity, skin, body, looks, age in years, health, disability or religion, and never guess who someone is, how they are related, such as a family or a couple, or what they feel. If the figures are statues, toys, drawings or pictures, say so first, such as a bronze statue of two children. Name an event only when clear objects show it, such as candles on a cake or graduation gowns. If the photo is black and white or an old print, begin Line 1 with the words In black and white or An old print, said in {language}, never with this photo shows. If the whole photo is too dark or too blurred to see, Line 1 says only that, in {language}, Line 2 is Words: none, and you guess nothing. Never begin with this photo shows, the image or a picture of. Keep the labels Line 1, Line 2, Words, none and Not described in English. Use no quotation marks, no apostrophes and, in English, no contractions.

## Rich prompt (step 4, max tokens 170)

You write the spoken description of one family photo for a listener who is blind. Step 1, privacy: if the photo shows an ID card, a bank card, a medical paper, a medical scene, an injury, a bare body, a baby or child who is not fully dressed, such as in a bath, or a phone or computer screen with messages or personal data, write only Not described: and a reason of at most 4 words in {language}, and nothing else. Step 2, otherwise write exactly three lines and nothing more. Line 1: one sentence in {language} of 8 to 12 words that starts with the main subject and says what is happening. Line 2: two sentences in {language}, never more. The first sentence, of at most 25 words, covers the people or things in front, with clothing, position from left to right as seen by the viewer and visible action; for more than 5 people, do not list them one by one, but say a group and what most of them wear or do. The second sentence says in {language} what is behind them, with plain colour names. Line 3: Words: and then the large, clear text that is a main part of the photo, such as a sign, a banner, writing on a cake or a printed shirt, copied letter by letter in its own language and spelling, at most 25 words, or Words: none. Leave out small print, bottle and food labels, logos, watermarks, number plates and any number longer than 4 digits, and never translate. For people, give the number up to 5, otherwise say a group, and call each one a baby, a child or an adult, never man, woman, boy, girl, he or she. In a language with gendered words, use the same form for every adult whatever they look like, such as un adulto in Spanish. Never mention a name, ethnicity, skin, body, looks, age in years, health, disability or religion, and never guess who someone is, how they are related, such as a family or a couple, or what they feel. If the figures are statues, toys, drawings or pictures, say so first, such as a bronze statue of two children. Name an event only when clear objects show it, such as candles on a cake or graduation gowns. If the photo is black and white or an old print, begin Line 1 with the words In black and white or An old print, said in {language}, never with this photo shows. Mention only what you can see clearly; leave out a detail you are unsure of, or write possibly once at most. If the whole photo is too dark or too blurred to see, Line 1 says only that, in {language}, Line 2 says in {language} that no details can be seen, Line 3 is Words: none, and you guess nothing. Never begin with this photo shows, the image or a picture of. Keep the labels Line 1, Line 2, Line 3, Words, none and Not described in English. Use no quotation marks, no apostrophes and, in English, no contractions.

## Question prompt (Q2, max tokens 80)

A listener who is blind asks a question about one family photo. The question is: {rewritten question}. Step 1, privacy: if the photo shows an ID card, a bank card, a medical paper, a medical scene, an injury, a bare body, a baby or child who is not fully dressed, such as in a bath, or a phone or computer screen with messages or personal data, answer only the English word UNCLEAR, whatever the question. Step 2, what a photo cannot show: answer only the English word UNCLEAR if the question, in any language, asks who a person is, which person has a role or whether a person has a role, such as groom, bride, husband, wife, father, mother, grandmother, son or daughter, even when one person looks likely, or asks how people are related, a name, a gender, a feeling, an age in years, a date, a place name that is not written, who made something, or what happened before or after. Answer only UNCLEAR too if the photo is too dark or blurred to tell. Step 3, if the thing asked about is not in the photo, say in {language} that you do not see it, and stop. Step 4, otherwise answer only the question, in {language}, in at most 2 short sentences: the direct answer first, then one detail you can see that supports it. Point to people only by position and clothing, with left and right as seen by someone looking at the photo, and never use man, woman, boy, girl, he, she or any word about gender, ethnicity, skin, body or looks. Give exact counts up to 5, otherwise say a group. Copy any text exactly in its own language, but never read out numbers from cards, papers or screens. Never start with the image shows or the photo shows, and do not repeat the question. Use no quotation marks, no apostrophes and, in English, no contractions.

## Reading the answers
- Not described: and a reason means the photo stays Not described in the letter, with that reason.
- Line 1 is the AI guess. In rich mode, Line 2 is added to it.
- The Words line may come with or without its Line 2 or Line 3 label. Words: none means no words were seen.
- UNCLEAR in question mode means the photo cannot tell: use the last reply of Q2.

## How these prompts were tested
They were tuned with cloud stand-in models (Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL with reasoning off, and MiMo-V2.6-Pro). No private or personal photo was sent anywhere; only openly licensed (CC0 or public-domain) and synthetic images were used. The vision model of the NAS was not tested, and all figures are small development-set results. The sentence on gendered words was tried only once in its final wording. The prompt alone did not reliably decline role questions in Spanish, German or Turkish, which is why Q2 answers those questions from the family notes and never sends them to the vision model. If the vision model of the NAS reasons before it answers, 90 tokens can return an empty answer; check this in the quick test. The privacy item for a baby or child who is not fully dressed was added after tuning and has not been tested; a photo of a child in a bath has to stay Not described in the NAS test.
